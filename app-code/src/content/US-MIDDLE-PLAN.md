- Never set `tier` or `paper`.

## US conventions (all subjects)
- **American English**: color, fiber, sulfur, aluminum, liter, meter, vaporize, analyze, organize, behavior, center. No emojis.
- Students are 12-14 years old: rigorous but age-appropriate, comparable to state end-of-grade tests (SBAC, PARCC/NJSLA, STAAR, NY State) and NAEP.
- Math: real Common Core codes in `standards`, e.g. `"7.RP.A.2"`, `"8.EE.C.7"`. Money as `$\\$12.50$`.
- Science: NGSS performance expectations in `standards`, e.g. `"MS-LS1-1"`, `"MS-PS1-5"`, plus a practice when relevant, e.g. `"SEP: Analyzing and Interpreting Data"`. Use SI units (meters, kilograms, g/cm³ as `$\\text{g/cm}^3$`, degrees Celsius). Command words: Identify, Describe, Explain, Calculate, Predict, Compare, Construct an explanation, Use evidence, Evaluate, Design. Include CER (claim, evidence, reasoning) items and data/graph reading described in words.
- ELA: Common Core codes in `standards`, e.g. `"RL.7.1"`, `"RI.8.8"`, `"W.7.1"`, `"L.8.1"`. Mark codes are AO1-AO5 with these US meanings (shown to students):
  AO1 read closely and cite textual evidence; AO2 analyze craft, structure and word choice; AO3 compare and connect texts; AO4 write for task, purpose and audience; AO5 language conventions (grammar, usage, mechanics, spelling).
  Levels for extended answers use a 4-level rubric, highest first, e.g. "Level 4" "7-8" (thorough, precise, well-supported), "Level 3" "5-6" (clear, adequate), "Level 2" "3-4" (partial, general), "Level 1" "1-2" (minimal). The highest level's upper mark MUST equal the marks of that question/part. q3 4-8 marks; q4 structured 3-4 parts, total 12-24 marks.
  Extracts: public-domain American and British texts only (published before 1929: Twain, Poe, Jack London, O. Henry, Alcott, Dickinson, Whitman, Frost's early poems, Longfellow, Douglass, Lincoln, Kipling, Dickens, Stevenson), quoted ONLY if you know them verbatim with high confidence; otherwise write ORIGINAL passages with `author: "Written for Akademik Space"`. Informational passages are usually original. The `$` character is FORBIDDEN in ELA files (write "dollars").

## Unit plan ((a) and (b) are the two concepts of each lesson)

### us-math-g7 Grade 7 Math
- unit-1 "Ratios and Proportional Relationships": 1-1 Unit Rates and Proportional Relationships (a) unit rates with fractions, complex fractions (7.RP.A.1) (b) recognizing proportional relationships and the constant of proportionality in tables, graphs and equations $y = kx$ (7.RP.A.2); 1-2 Percent Problems (a) tax, tips, markups and discounts (7.RP.A.3) (b) percent increase, decrease and percent error (7.RP.A.3); 1-3 Applying Proportions (a) scale drawings and scale factor (7.G.A.1) (b) simple interest and commission (7.RP.A.3)
- unit-2 "The Number System, Expressions and Equations": 2-1 Operations with Rational Numbers (a) adding and subtracting integers and rational numbers on the number line, absolute value as distance (7.NS.A.1) (b) multiplying and dividing rational numbers, terminating and repeating decimals (7.NS.A.2); 2-2 Expressions (a) equivalent expressions: distributive property, factoring, combining like terms (7.EE.A.1, 7.EE.A.2) (b) multi-step problems with rational numbers and estimation (7.EE.B.3); 2-3 Equations and Inequalities (a) two-step equations $px + q = r$ and $p(x + q) = r$ (7.EE.B.4a) (b) two-step inequalities, graphing solution sets (7.EE.B.4b)
- unit-3 "Geometry, Statistics and Probability": 3-1 Circles and Angles (a) circumference and area of circles (7.G.B.4) (b) supplementary, complementary, vertical and adjacent angles, writing equations (7.G.B.5); 3-2 Area, Surface Area and Volume (a) area of composite figures, surface area and volume of right prisms (7.G.B.6) (b) drawing triangles from conditions and cross-sections of solids (7.G.A.2, 7.G.A.3); 3-3 Statistics and Probability (a) random sampling, inferences, comparing two populations with mean and MAD (7.SP.A.1, 7.SP.B.3, 7.SP.B.4) (b) probability of simple and compound events, sample spaces, tree diagrams (7.SP.C.5-8)

### us-science-g7 Grade 7 Science
- unit-1 "Life Science: Cells, Organisms and Ecosystems": 1-1 Cells (a) cell theory, plant and animal cell organelles and their functions (MS-LS1-1, MS-LS1-2) (b) unicellular and multicellular organisms, microscopes and magnification; 1-2 Body Systems and Energy (a) levels of organization and interacting body systems (MS-LS1-3) (b) photosynthesis and cellular respiration (MS-LS1-6, MS-LS1-7); 1-3 Ecosystems (a) food chains, food webs and energy pyramids, the 10% rule (MS-LS2-3) (b) competition, predation, symbiosis and limiting factors (MS-LS2-1, MS-LS2-2)
- unit-2 "Physical Science: Matter and Its Interactions": 2-1 Structure of Matter (a) atoms, elements, molecules, compounds and mixtures (MS-PS1-1) (b) density $\\rho = \\frac{m}{V}$ and identifying substances by properties (MS-PS1-2); 2-2 States and Changes (a) particle motion, states of matter and thermal energy (MS-PS1-4) (b) physical and chemical changes and evidence of a chemical reaction (MS-PS1-2); 2-3 Chemical Reactions (a) conservation of mass, counting atoms and balancing simple equations (MS-PS1-5) (b) endothermic and exothermic processes, designing a hot or cold pack (MS-PS1-6)
- unit-3 "Earth Science: Earth's Systems": 3-1 Rocks and Plates (a) the rock cycle and Earth materials (MS-ESS2-1) (b) plate tectonics and evidence from fossils, rocks and seafloor (MS-ESS2-3); 3-2 Water and Weather (a) the water cycle driven by the Sun and gravity (MS-ESS2-4) (b) air masses, fronts and weather prediction (MS-ESS2-5); 3-3 Resources and Hazards (a) uneven distribution of natural resources (MS-ESS3-1) (b) natural hazards and human impact, per-capita consumption (MS-ESS3-2, MS-ESS3-3)

### us-ela-g7 Grade 7 English Language Arts
- unit-1 "Reading Literature": 1-1 Evidence and Theme (a) citing several pieces of textual evidence and making inferences (RL.7.1) (b) determining theme and writing an objective summary (RL.7.2); 1-2 Story Elements and Language (a) how setting shapes characters and plot (RL.7.3) (b) figurative language, connotation and sound devices (RL.7.4); 1-3 Point of View and Structure (a) contrasting characters' points of view, dramatic irony (RL.7.6) (b) how the form or structure of a poem or drama contributes to meaning (RL.7.5)
- unit-2 "Reading Informational Text": 2-1 Central Ideas and Structure (a) two or more central ideas and an objective summary (RI.7.2) (b) text structures: chronological, compare/contrast, cause/effect, problem/solution (RI.7.5); 2-2 Purpose and Argument (a) author's purpose and point of view (RI.7.6) (b) tracing and evaluating claims, reasons and evidence (RI.7.8); 2-3 Words and Comparison (a) word meaning from context and Greek and Latin roots (L.7.4) (b) comparing how two authors present the same topic (RI.7.9)
- unit-3 "Writing and Language": 3-1 Argument and Informative Writing (a) argument: claim, reasons, evidence, counterclaim (W.7.1) (b) informative writing: organizing ideas, precise language (W.7.2); 3-2 Narrative Writing (a) narrative techniques: dialogue, pacing, description (W.7.3) (b) transitions, formal style and conclusions (W.7.1c, W.7.2c); 3-3 Conventions (a) phrases and clauses; simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences; misplaced modifiers (L.7.1) (b) commas with coordinate adjectives, spelling, and concise language (L.7.2, L.7.3)

### us-math-g8 Grade 8 Math
- unit-1 "The Number System and Exponents": 1-1 Rational and Irrational Numbers (a) classifying real numbers, converting repeating decimals to fractions (8.NS.A.1) (b) approximating irrational numbers, ordering on the number line (8.NS.A.2); 1-2 Exponents and Scientific Notation (a) integer exponent laws (8.EE.A.1) (b) scientific notation: writing, comparing and operating (8.EE.A.3, 8.EE.A.4); 1-3 Roots and Linear Equations (a) square roots and cube roots, solving $x^2 = p$ and $x^3 = p$ (8.EE.A.2) (b) linear equations in one variable with one, no or infinitely many solutions, variables on both sides (8.EE.C.7)
- unit-2 "Linear Relationships and Functions": 2-1 Slope and Proportional Relationships (a) graphing proportional relationships, unit rate as slope, comparing representations (8.EE.B.5) (b) slope from similar triangles, $y = mx + b$ (8.EE.B.6); 2-2 Functions (a) defining, evaluating and comparing functions in tables, graphs, equations and words (8.F.A.1, 8.F.A.2) (b) linear vs nonlinear functions, constructing linear models and interpreting rate of change and initial value, describing graphs qualitatively (8.F.A.3, 8.F.B.4, 8.F.B.5); 2-3 Systems and Bivariate Data (a) systems of two linear equations: graphing, substitution, elimination, applied problems (8.EE.C.8) (b) scatter plots, association, lines of best fit, two-way tables (8.SP.A.1-4)
- unit-3 "Geometry": 3-1 Transformations (a) translations, reflections and rotations and their properties, coordinates (8.G.A.1, 8.G.A.3) (b) dilations, congruence and similarity through transformations (8.G.A.2, 8.G.A.4); 3-2 Angles and the Pythagorean Theorem (a) angle sum of triangles, exterior angles, parallel lines cut by a transversal, angle-angle similarity (8.G.A.5) (b) Pythagorean Theorem and its converse, distance between points (8.G.B.6-8); 3-3 Volume (a) volume of cylinders and cones (8.G.C.9) (b) volume of spheres and composite solids (8.G.C.9)

### us-science-g8 Grade 8 Science
- unit-1 "Physical Science: Forces, Motion and Energy": 1-1 Motion and Forces (a) speed, distance-time graphs, balanced and unbalanced forces (MS-PS2-2) (b) Newton's three laws, $F = ma$ qualitatively and simple calculations (MS-PS2-1, MS-PS2-2); 1-2 Gravity, Electricity and Magnetism (a) gravitational forces depend on mass and distance (MS-PS2-4) (b) electric and magnetic forces, electromagnets and fields (MS-PS2-3, MS-PS2-5); 1-3 Energy (a) kinetic energy $KE = \\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ and potential energy, relationships with mass and speed (MS-PS3-1, MS-PS3-2) (b) energy transfer, conservation, thermal energy and insulation design (MS-PS3-3, MS-PS3-4, MS-PS3-5)
- unit-2 "Waves and Information": 2-1 Wave Properties (a) amplitude, wavelength, frequency, wave speed $v = f\\lambda$ (MS-PS4-1) (b) mechanical waves and sound (MS-PS4-1, MS-PS4-2); 2-2 Light (a) the electromagnetic spectrum (MS-PS4-2) (b) reflection, refraction, absorption and transmission of light (MS-PS4-2); 2-3 Digital Signals (a) analog vs digital signals, encoding information (MS-PS4-3) (b) reliability of digital transmission, evaluating designs
- unit-3 "Life and Earth: Heredity, Evolution and the Universe": 3-1 Heredity (a) genes, mutations and proteins (MS-LS3-1) (b) sexual vs asexual reproduction, Punnett squares and probability (MS-LS3-2); 3-2 Evolution (a) the fossil record, anatomical and embryological evidence (MS-LS4-1, MS-LS4-2, MS-LS4-3) (b) natural selection, adaptation and artificial selection (MS-LS4-4, MS-LS4-5, MS-LS4-6); 3-3 Earth in Space (a) the Earth-Sun-Moon system: moon phases, eclipses, seasons (MS-ESS1-1) (b) gravity in the solar system and galaxies, scale properties of solar system objects, the geologic time scale (MS-ESS1-2, MS-ESS1-3, MS-ESS1-4)

### us-ela-g8 Grade 8 English Language Arts
- unit-1 "Reading Literature": 1-1 Evidence and Theme (a) citing the strongest textual evidence (RL.8.1) (b) theme development, its relationship to characters, setting and plot, and objective summary (RL.8.2); 1-2 Dialogue, Incidents and Word Choice (a) how lines of dialogue or incidents propel action, reveal character or provoke a decision (RL.8.3) (b) word meanings, analogies and allusions, impact of word choice on tone (RL.8.4); 1-3 Structure, Point of View and Sources (a) comparing structures of two texts, dramatic irony, suspense and humor through point of view (RL.8.5, RL.8.6) (b) how a modern work draws on myths, traditional stories or religious works (RL.8.9)
- unit-2 "Reading Informational Text": 2-1 Central Idea and Connections (a) central idea development and objective summary (RI.8.2) (b) connections and distinctions between individuals, ideas or events: analogies, comparisons, categories (RI.8.3); 2-2 Structure and Point of View (a) structure of a specific paragraph and the role of key sentences (RI.8.5) (b) author's point of view and how the author acknowledges and responds to conflicting viewpoints (RI.8.6); 2-3 Argument and Conflicting Texts (a) evaluating arguments, recognizing irrelevant evidence (RI.8.8) (b) analyzing two texts that disagree on facts or interpretation (RI.8.9)
- unit-3 "Writing and Language": 3-1 Argument and Informative Writing (a) argument: distinguishing claims from opposing claims, credible sources (W.8.1) (b) informative writing: organization, formatting, precise language, varied transitions (W.8.2); 3-2 Narrative and Research (a) narrative techniques: point of view, reflection, pacing, relationships among experiences (W.8.3) (b) research: gathering relevant information, quoting vs paraphrasing, avoiding plagiarism (W.8.7, W.8.8); 3-3 Conventions (a) verbals (gerunds, participles, infinitives), active and passive voice, verb moods (L.8.1) (b) commas, ellipses and dashes; shifts in voice and mood (L.8.1d, L.8.2)
